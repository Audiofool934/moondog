Moondog is on the air. Just for you.

A radio host that learns your taste from the history your music services already keep, talks music with you, and plays what fits.

It starts with what you've played

Bring your history from the services you already use. Moondog shows you what's inside before anything is saved, never touches your files, and changes nothing if you import the same file twice.

Spotify
Recent plays after you connect, or your whole history from the ZIP Spotify emails you.
Apple Music
The Library.xml you export from Music on a Mac.
YouTube Music
A Google Takeout export.
Last.fm
Your scrobbles and loved tracks, by username.
ListenBrainz
A saved listens file.
QQ Music and NetEase
Any public playlist, from its share link.

From that, Moondog learns your taste. It keeps what you played apart from what the plays can't prove: a song you play a lot isn't always a song that matters to you. Open any song or artist to see why it's there, and tell Moondog when it got you wrong.

It plays where you listen

On Spotify, on any of your devices. On Apple Music, as whole songs with a subscription on macOS and 30-second previews elsewhere. On NetEase Music, for listeners in mainland China.

Moondog sees what's already playing on Spotify or in the Music app on macOS, and keeps one thing playing at a time. The record turns while the music plays. Click its arm, and Moondog picks something for right now.

Bring your own model

Moondog talks through the model you pick: GLM, Kimi, DeepSeek, Grok, GPT, Claude, Gemini, or anything on OpenRouter. ChatGPT sign-in works too. Your taste and your history work without one.

Each step shows in the conversation as it happens, and every recording is checked in the catalog before it plays or goes in the queue.

The dark side of the moon What leaves your computer

Moondog names what play counts can never tell it about you. It is just as plain about where your data goes.

  • Your history, your profile and your conversations stay on your computer.
  • When you build a profile, Moondog sends a compact summary of your music evidence to the model you picked. When you talk, your messages go to that model too.
  • Spotify, Apple Music and NetEase see only what you connect and ask for. Moondog makes playlists only when you ask, and never posts, deletes or spends money.
  • For the lyric on the sleeve, Moondog asks LRCLIB for the words to a song you played, and sends nothing else.
  • To find updates, Moondog reads the list of releases. It sends nothing about you.

Get Moondog

For Mac

Open the download and drag Moondog to Applications. It's notarized by Apple. When a new version is out, an Update button appears, and nothing downloads until you click it.

In a terminal

On macOS or Linux, with Node.js 22.19 or newer. The terminal and the desktop app share your profile, your sign-ins and your conversations.

npm install --global @audiofool/moondog
moondog

moondog update keeps it current, and moondog demo --offline shows Moondog at work on made-up data, with no sign-in. The terminal guide covers every import format and model setting.

Moondog takes its name from Alan Freed, who went on the air in Cleveland in 1951 as the Moondog and helped give rock and roll its name.

It is open source under the Apache 2.0 license, and built on Pi. Lyrics on the sleeve come from LRCLIB. The listener in the window above is made up, and its music was made with Stability AI's Stable Audio 3.
